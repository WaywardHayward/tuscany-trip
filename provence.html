We loved Saint-Paul for the perched stone and the green calm. The Luberon is exactly that magic, with room to roam — and a pool to come home to.

This is the most Saint-Paul of all the trips: a string of honey-and-ochre villages on their hilltops — Gordes, Roussillon, Ménerbes — a Provençal mas with a pool to come back to, lavender rolling up to a Cistercian abbey, and Saturday markets piled with melons and tomatoes. The grand Roman south is on the doorstep too: Avignon's papal palace, the arena at Arles where Van Gogh painted, and the impossible three tiers of the Pont du Gard. The fast train drops you in Avignon in an afternoon; a little hire car handles the hilltop lanes from there. Vegetarian heaven, gentle days, and a different perched village every time you stir.

The plan

A farmhouse in the Luberon, near Gordes

Not a hotel in town — a mas, a Provençal stone farmhouse with a pool and a garden, tucked into the hills within reach of Gordes. The closest thing there is to those Saint-Paul photos, with a different perched village over every rise. A small hire car makes the lanes easy; otherwise you needn't move.

Good to know

The practical bits

Getting there on rails

Quick and easy: one fast train almost the whole way to the edge of the Luberon.

  • Edinburgh → London → Paris · LNER ~4h20 + Eurostar ~2h20 · overnight in Paris
  • Paris → Avignon · direct TGV ~3h40 down the Rhône valley
  • Avignon → the Luberon · ~45 min by car — pick up a small hire car at the station for the hilltop lanes

Getting around

Avignon, Arles and Nîmes are all dead easy by train, and the Pont du Gard is a short hop from there. The perched villages, the abbey and the markets, though, really want a car — the cleanest answer is the Tuscany one: long-haul on rails, a small hire car just for the Provençal leg, dropped before the train home.

Eating well, meat-free

Provence is a vegetarian's paradise of sun and vegetables:

  • Ratatouille and a tian of summer vegetables
  • Soupe au pistou; tapenade on good bread
  • Goat cheese, ripe tomatoes, olives and herbs
  • Melon, figs, and lavender-honey ice cream

When to go

For the lavender you want the windows either side of midsummer. Aim for late June or July for the abbey rows in full purple; the villages are warm and golden, the markets at their best, and the pool very much in use.

Back to Saint-Paul, scaled up?

A pool in a Provençal garden, a perched village over every hill, lavender and Roman stone — the trip the whole idea began with. Back to the other options whenever.
