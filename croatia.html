The quiet, green side of Croatia

Croatia without the crowds: Istria, the green peninsula up north, where it feels a little like Italy crossed with the south of France — and you can still get there by sea.

Base in Rovinj, a honey-and-ochre Venetian old town that climbs out of the water to a single bell-tower, and let the peninsula come to you. Roman Pula with its great amphitheatre, the gold mosaics of Poreč, hilltop towns like Motovun and Grožnjan in truffle country, the islands of Brijuni, and wild swimming off Cape Kamenjak. Warm, clear water; cobbled lanes like home; food that's a vegetarian's dream of truffle, olive oil, fresh pasta and garden veg. The romantic way in is the rail spine through France and Italy, then a fast catamaran across from Venice.

The plan

One base, on the prettiest harbour

No need to move on: Rovinj sits in the middle of Istria with everything an easy day out — Pula and Poreč down the coast, the hill towns just inland, the islands a short boat away. It's the loveliest old town on this coast, all sea-worn ochre and Venetian shutters, and a joy to come home to each evening.

The practical bits

Getting there on rails (then the boat)

The long romantic way: rails across France and Italy, then a fast catamaran over the top of the Adriatic.

  • Edinburgh → London → Paris · LNER ~4h10 + Eurostar ~2h20 · overnight in Paris
  • Paris → Milan · Frecciarossa ~7h · overnight in Milan
  • Milan → Venice · ~2h25, then catamaran Venice → Rovinj ~3h

Around Istria

Rovinj is the hub. The summer catamaran (Venezia Lines / Kompas, roughly May–October, foot passengers) crosses from Venice's San Basilio terminal. It runs only a few days a week, so we'd build the trip around a sailing. Within Istria, buses link Rovinj with Pula (~45–60 min) and Poreč (~45 min); Brijuni is a short boat from Fažana, and the hill towns and Kamenjak are easy as a small-group day tour from Rovinj — so there's no need for a car at any point.

Eating well, meat-free

Istria is a vegetarian's dream:

  • Fuži and pljukanci pasta with truffle or wild asparagus
  • Maneštra — the hearty Istrian bean-and-veg soup
  • Local olive oils, sheep's cheese, ripe tomatoes and figs
  • Gelato on the harbour and a cooling watermelon in the heat

Sea, shade & siesta

The Adriatic is warm and wonderfully clear by summer, so build the days around a swim and keep the hottest hours for shade — the old-town lanes, a cool basilica, or a hill town where the air moves. Croatia uses the euro now, and most of Istria speaks Italian as readily as Croatian.
