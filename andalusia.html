This is the one you actually asked for: Ikos itself — and reached, the whole way, without ever boarding a plane.

Ikos Andalusia sits on its own stretch of beach near Estepona, on the soft western end of the Costa del Sol with Marbella down the road and the white-village hills rising behind. It's the easiest holiday there is: a five-star, all-inclusive resort where the rooms, the pools, the spa, the beach service and nine restaurants are simply there, sorted, the moment you arrive. Getting to it is the only bit of planning — and even that's a pleasure: Eurostar to Paris, a fast run down to Barcelona, the high-speed line along the coast to Málaga, then a short transfer to the sand. Do nothing for a week, or slip out to Ronda's gorge, Moorish Málaga and the whitewashed hill towns whenever you fancy.

The plan

Ikos Andalusia, Estepona

A five-star, all-inclusive resort on its own beach near Estepona, under an hour from Málaga. Nine restaurants with menus from Michelin-starred chefs, a big spa, a run of pools and a private stretch of sand — you never have to leave, but Ronda, Málaga and the hill towns are a short hop when you want them.

It's all-in in the fullest sense: the restaurants, room service, the spa's thermal suites and even a car for a day to go exploring are part of the deal. The one real choice is which room grade you settle into — that sets your view and your price:

Good to know

The practical bits

Getting there — all by rail

The whole point: no planes. A lovely run down through France and Spain, then a short transfer to the sand.

  • Edinburgh → London → Paris · LNER ~4h20 + Eurostar ~2h20 · overnight in Paris
  • Paris → Barcelona · one high-speed train ~6h30 · a night in Barcelona
  • Barcelona → Málaga · AVE ~5h50, then a ~1h transfer to the resort (the coast railway stops at Fuengirola, so the last leg is by road — Ikos arranges it)

The resort & getting around

Ikos handles the transfer from Málaga, and inside everything's a short walk or a buggy ride — beach, spa, pools and the restaurants all on site. The all-inclusive even includes a car for a day, so a run up to Ronda, Casares or the Caminito del Rey is easy; Málaga itself you can also reach on the local coast train. No hire car needed.

Eating well, meat-free

Andalusia is kind to vegetarians, and the resort's nine kitchens even more so:

  • Salmorejo and gazpacho — chilled tomato soups
  • Espinacas con garbanzos — spinach with chickpeas
  • Berenjenas con miel — fried aubergine with cane honey
  • Bread, olives, oranges and almonds; churros and ice cream

The splurge, and when

This is the indulgent, no-effort one — everything's done for you, so it sits at the top of the budget in peak. Aim for June or September: the prices and crowds ease, the sea is warm, and the Costa del Sol's fierce midsummer heat softens to something lovely.

Just switch off?

A private beach, big pools, a spa and nine kitchens — all of it sorted, and all of it reached without an airport in sight. Back to the other options whenever.
