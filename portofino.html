Basically your beloved Côte d'Azur, carried over the border into Italy: pastel harbours, coastal paths, and pesto on everything.

Base in calm Santa Margherita and let the coast come to you: ferry round to Portofino's famous little harbour, boat to the abbey at San Fruttuoso, train down to the Cinque Terre, and graze on focaccia, farinata and trofie al pesto the whole way. Colourful perched villages like home, coast like the south of France, and warm but bearable in summer with the sea breeze. A vegetarian's dream, all pesto and focaccia and sea air.

Good to know

The practical bits

Getting there on rails

A kinder rail trip than the deep south, with one overnight:

  • Edinburgh → London → Paris · LNER ~4h10 + Eurostar ~2h20
  • Paris → Milan · Frecciarossa ~7h · overnight in Milan
  • Milan → Santa Margherita · ~2h direct, or via Genoa (1h27) + coastal hop ~35 min

Around the coast

Boat-and-train heaven. The coastal railway links Camogli, Santa Margherita, Rapallo, the Cinque Terre (~30–55 min) and Genoa; ferries hop you to Portofino and San Fruttuoso. Portofino has no station — it's bus 782 (~25 min), ferry, or the lovely coast path; San Fruttuoso is boat-or-foot only. Cinque Terre trails can close after rain, so check on the day.

Eating well, meat-free

Liguria is a vegetarian's dream:

  • Pesto alla genovese with trofie or trenette
  • Focaccia, focaccia di Recco, and farinata
  • Minestrone alla genovese, stuffed veg
  • Gelato and a lemon granita in the heat

Coffee, granita & sea air

Coffee on the seafront, a lemon granita in the heat, gelato on the walk back. The long passeggiata and a swim off the rocks do the rest.

Worth a flutter?

A pastel harbour by morning, a hidden cove by lunch, a clifftop village at dusk, and the sea the whole way. Back to the other options whenever.
