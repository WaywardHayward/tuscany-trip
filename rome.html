Rome you already know, done differently: past the big-ticket queues, into the green and the gardens.

You've done the Colosseum and the Vatican, so skip them this time. Base in Rome and go for the gentler, greener side — the vast Villa Doria Pamphilj park on your doorstep, the Gianicolo view, Trastevere evenings, Bernini and Caravaggio in the Borghese, the umbrella pines of the Appian Way — then day-trip to some of the finest gardens on earth: Tivoli's hundred fountains, the romantic ruin-garden of Ninfa, the monsters of Bomarzo. One honest note: Rome bakes in July and August, so spring or autumn is the kinder choice.

Good to know

The practical bits

Getting there on rails

Rome's a fair way south, so it's a two-dayer each way — lovely if you lean in, with an overnight in Turin:

  • Edinburgh → London → Paris · LNER ~4h10 + Eurostar ~2h20
  • Paris → Turin · TGV ~5h40 · overnight in Turin
  • Turin → Rome · Frecciarossa ~4h direct · or push Paris → Rome in a day ~11h via Milan (06:28 → 17:40)

Around Rome & Lazio

Rome's walkable with a handy metro. Tivoli is ~1h on regional trains from Tiburtina; Ostia Antica ~30 min on the Roma-Lido line. A couple of the gardens (Ninfa, Bomarzo) are simpler with a hire car or an organised tour. Note: the Borghese, Ostia and Villa Lante shut Mondays; Ninfa opens weekends only.

Eating well, meat-free

Roman cooking is a vegetarian's friend:

  • Cacio e pepe and pasta alla gricia (skip the guanciale)
  • Carciofi alla romana — those famous artichokes
  • Pizza al taglio, supplì, fiori di zucca
  • Maritozzi and gelato to finish

Coffee, gelato & the passeggiata

The coffee is taken very seriously and the gelato more so. An evening passeggiata past the floodlit ruins is the city at its best, and a granita keeps you going in the heat.

Tempting, eh?

Ruins by morning, a fountain-filled garden by afternoon, pizza by night, and two thousand years underfoot the whole time. Back to the other options whenever.
