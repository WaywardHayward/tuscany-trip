A slow green fortnight in Tuscany

One farmhouse with a pool, unpacked once. Everything else is an easy day out and home by dark.

~12 daysOne garden baseNever repackGardens & hill towns

The plan is simple: find a pool tucked into a garden, sink into it, and let Tuscany come to us at walking pace.

We loved the south of France for the perched stone villages, the art and the green calm. Tuscany has all of that with a different accent: one farmhouse-and-pool to come home to every single night, two of the great garden villas down the road, Florence for the art, and a string of hill towns that feel like Saint-Paul's Italian cousins. Vegetarian heaven. You unpack once on day one and never touch the suitcase again — every outing is a there-and-back, with the pool waiting at the end of it.

The practical bits

Getting there

The simple way: fly Edinburgh → Pisa (under 3 hours), then a 30-minute transfer to the base — in the pool the same afternoon.

Or, if the journey's part of the holiday, take the scenic rails down over two gentle days with a night in Paris:

  • Edinburgh → London · LNER, from ~4h10 (up to ~46 trains a day)
  • London → Paris · Eurostar ~2h20 · overnight near Gare de Lyon
  • Paris → Turin · TGV ~5h40 (e.g. Gare de Lyon 06:45 → Turin 12:23)
  • Turin → Florence · Frecciarossa ~2h · then Florence → Lucca regional ~1h20, hourly

Prefer one long push? Paris → Florence in a day runs ~9h35 via Milan (06:28 → 16:04). One thing to dodge: the Paris–Milan line closes for works ~11 Sept–9 Oct 2026, so travel before mid-September or after mid-October.

No car, all rails

You don't need a car, and we're not using one. Lucca, Florence, Pisa, Siena and San Gimignano are all easy on the train, and the two garden villas are a short taxi or local bus from Lucca. Pick a base near the station or with a shuttle and the whole trip runs on rails, with just the odd local taxi for the last door-to-door hop.

Eating well, meat-free

Tuscany is a vegetarian's happy place. Reliable joys to look for:

  • Ribollita and pappa al pomodoro
  • Pici with cacio e pepe or garlic
  • Panzanella, white beans in good oil
  • Pecorino, fresh focaccia, proper tomatoes

Coffee, gelato & the evening ritual

Italy is built for slow evenings. The passeggiata, an espresso at the bar, gelato on the walk home, and a long sit on the terrace as the light goes do all the work. Long golden evenings, every one of them.
