Helen's own summer run: four cities, one rail pass of a fortnight, north to south and back again.

Not a “which shall we pick” this time — this one's booked. Down the East Coast on LNER, across on Eurostar for a night in Paris, then the long high-speed haul to Florence for three nights of art and Renaissance. On to the Bay of Naples and four slow nights in Sorrento — lemon groves, Capri, Pompeii — before turning north for a couple of nights in Milan, a last evening in Paris, and home. Every leg on a train.

The rail legs, in order

The whole loop is booked on trains — the long days are the reward, not the chore:

  • Home → London → Paris · LNER + Eurostar, night in Paris
  • Paris → Florence · 07:50 → 17:04, high-speed via the Alps
  • Florence → Naples → Sorrento · ~3h30 + ~1h15 on the Circumvesuviana
  • Sorrento → Naples → Milan · Frecciarossa the length of Italy
  • Milan → Paris · 06:25 → 13:13, back over the Alps
  • Paris → London → home · Eurostar + LNER

Booking & changes

Reserve the Eurostar and the French high-speed legs (Paris–Florence, Milan–Paris) as early as you can — fares climb and seats sell out. The Italian Frecciarossa legs are flexible and frequent. One to watch: the Paris–Milan line closes for works ~11 Sept–9 Oct 2026, but this trip runs in late July, well clear of it.

Around the Bay of Naples

The Circumvesuviana threads Naples → Herculaneum → Pompeii Scavi (~35 min) → Sorrento (~65 min, every 30 min) — cheap and frequent, if a bit rough; the faster Campania Express is worth the few extra euros. Hydrofoils reach Capri in ~20–25 min from Sorrento.

Eating well, meat-free

Vegetarian heaven the whole way down:

  • Florence — ribollita, pappa al pomodoro, pecorino
  • The bay — pizza margherita, parmigiana di melanzane, lemon everything
  • Milan — risotto alla milanese and a proper aperitivo
  • Paris, top and tail — the good bakery on both nights
