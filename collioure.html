A painted Catalan harbour for a base, and the whole Côte Vermeille to wander: the coast where Matisse invented Fauvism, coves to swim in, and day-trips that come to you by train.

Collioure is the little port that Matisse and Derain set on fire with colour in 1905 — pink and ochre houses stacked above a curved bay, a royal castle on the water, and the Notre-Dame-des-Anges bell-tower standing right out in the sea. Take one characterful villa with a pool and let the painted coast unspool around it: Maillol's sculptures and a snorkel trail at Banyuls, the Cubism museum at Céret, the Catalan capital at Perpignan, and Dalí's whitewashed Cadáques over the border — every one reachable on the Côte Vermeille railway, a little bus or a coastal boat. Hot, bright and slow, with the sea always a few steps down — and a vegetarian's run of market peaches, figs, grilled veg and crème catalane.

    Banyuls & the marine reserve

    A few minutes down the line to Banyuls: Aristide Maillol's home town, his serene bronzes about the seafront, and a marked snorkel trail through the underwater nature reserve. Sculpture in the morning, swimming after lunch.

    railsnorkel
  • 05

    A slow day at home

    After three days on the go, a day off the sightseeing. A lie-in, the morning market for peaches and figs, the villa pool through the hot hours, and a cove in the late afternoon. Nowhere to be.

    restpool
  • 06

    Céret, the Mecca of Cubism

    Inland by bus to Céret, where Picasso, Braque and Soutine worked: a lovely Musée d'Art Moderne, plane-tree squares, the great single-arch Pont du Diable, and the famous early cherries if the season's right.

    busart
  • 07

    Perpignan, the Catalan capital

    A quick train to the old capital of Roussillon: the Palace of the Kings of Majorca, Le Castillet gate, the Hyacinthe Rigaud museum, and the covered market for lunch. Properly Catalan, sardanes and all.

    railcity
  • 08

    Port-Vendres & a coastal walk

    One stop down to the working fishing harbour of Port-Vendres, then the cliff path out towards Cap Béar — lighthouses, hidden coves and the whole bay of Collioure laid out behind you. An easy half-day on foot, a swim to finish.

    walkcoves
  • 09

    Over the border to Cadáques

    Across into Spain by train-and-connection or a coastal boat to Dalí's whitewashed village under Cap de Creus — his house at Portlligat, the wild headland, a Mediterranean cove for a swim. Figueres and its Theatre-Museum are on the same line if the surrealism calls.

    boatDalí
  • 10

    A long beach day at Argèles

    Next stop north for kilometres of soft sand and shallow warm water — the easy, lazy beach day. Umbrella, a book, a long swim, an ice cream, and the train home along the shore when the light goes gold.

    beachswim
  • 11

    Collioure encore

    The walk up to Fort Saint-Elme for the view back over the red roofs and blue bay, a last potter round the easels and the market, and a final swim from the cove before the harbour lamps come on. The town you came to know.

    viewslast swim
  • Then turn for home, rolling the coast train back to Perpignan and the long rails north — salt in your hair and a bell-tower in the camera roll.

Good to know

The practical bits

Getting there on rails

All the way by train, with one easy night in Paris:

  • Edinburgh → London → Paris · LNER ~4h10 + Eurostar ~2h20 · overnight in Paris
  • Paris → Carcassonne · direct TGV ~6h31, or ~5h51 via Toulouse
  • or Paris → Toulouse → Carcassonne · TGV ~4h25 + TER ~1h (37 trains a day on the last leg)

Around the region

Carcassonne is the hub. The coast and Narbonne are quick TER hops (Narbonne ~30–35 min, frequent); Collioure ~2h30–3h via Narbonne; Albi ~2h via Toulouse. The Canal du Midi towpath and the lower town are on foot or by hired bike. The Cathar castles and the smaller villages (Lagrasse, Minerve) are reached by a guided minibus day tour from Carcassonne — the one part that leaves the rails, but still no car of your own.

Eating well, meat-free

The sunny south-west market table:

  • Tomato, melon, peach and fig from the morning market
  • Ratatouille, pan-fried ceps and girolles, stuffed veg
  • Salade catalane, escalivada and tapenade near the coast
  • Tarte aux pommes, and a pain au chocolat for the towpath

Heat, shade & sea air

Inland Languedoc gets properly hot at midday, so do the ramparts and castles early or late and save the shaded canal towpath or a swim for the middle of the day. The coast (Collioure, Narbonne-Plage) is a few degrees kinder with its sea breeze — an easy escape whenever the city bakes.

Storybook enough?

A floodlit fortress by night, a castle on a crag by lunch, a tree-shaded canal at your own pace, and the Mediterranean an hour away. Back to the other options whenever.
