A storybook walled city for a base, and the whole sun-baked Languedoc to wander: Cathar castles, a tree-lined canal, and a quick hop to the sea.

Carcassonne's double ramparts and turrets look drawn by a child — and you can sleep inside them. From that one base it all comes to you by train and towpath: the red-brick cathedral city of Albi, the plane-tree shade of the Canal du Midi, the clifftop Cathar castles, golden villages like Lagrasse and Minerve, and the little Fauvist harbour of Collioure on the Mediterranean. Hot, honeyed and slow, with a sea breeze whenever you want one — and a vegetarian's dream of market veg, olives, tomatoes and tarte.

  • 05

    A slow one in the lower town

    After two days of ramparts and cathedrals, a day off the sightseeing. A lie-in, the Saturday market on Place Carnot, a long lunch and a wander of the Bastide's lanes — or simply the garden and a book. Nowhere to be.

    restmarket
  • 06

    The Canal du Midi

    A slow day on the 17th-century canal: walk or hire bikes along the plane-tree towpath, watch the boats work a lock, picnic by the great Pont-canal aqueduct. Shade, water and birdsong.

    canalwalk
  • 07

    Cathar castles on the crags

    Out into the wild Corbières for the vertiginous ruins of Peyrepertuse and Quéribus, or the four towers of Lastours right above Carcassonne. Big skies, big drops, near-empty roads.

    castlesviews
  • 08

    Lagrasse & the golden villages

    One of France's "plus beaux villages": a humpback bridge, an old abbey and stone lanes in the Corbières hills. Or the gorge-top village of Minerve, clinging above its river canyon.

    villageslow
  • 09

    Down to the sea

    An hour or so by train to the Mediterranean: the painted harbour of Collioure that the Fauvists loved, with its bell-tower in the water — or grand Narbonne with its half-built cathedral and Roman road. Swim, then home on the breeze.

    coastswim

The plan

One base, the whole region around it

Carcassonne sits right on the main line, so Albi, the Canal du Midi, the Cathar castles and the coast are all easy days out from one bag-drop. The treat is staying in or right beneath the medieval Cité, so the walls are floodlit on your evening stroll and quiet again before the coaches arrive.

Good to know

The practical bits

Getting there on rails

All the way by train, with one easy night in Paris:

  • Edinburgh → London → Paris · LNER ~4h10 + Eurostar ~2h20 · overnight in Paris
  • Paris → Carcassonne · direct TGV ~6h31, or ~5h51 via Toulouse
  • or Paris → Toulouse → Carcassonne · TGV ~4h25 + TER ~1h (37 trains a day on the last leg)

Around the region

Carcassonne is the hub. The coast and Narbonne are quick TER hops (Narbonne ~30–35 min, frequent); Collioure ~2h30–3h via Narbonne; Albi ~2h via Toulouse. The Canal du Midi towpath and the lower town are on foot or by hired bike. The Cathar castles and the smaller villages (Lagrasse, Minerve) are reached by a guided minibus day tour from Carcassonne — the one part that leaves the rails, but still no car of your own.

Eating well, meat-free

The sunny south-west market table:

  • Tomato, melon, peach and fig from the morning market
  • Ratatouille, pan-fried ceps and girolles, stuffed veg
  • Salade catalane, escalivada and tapenade near the coast
  • Tarte aux pommes, and a pain au chocolat for the towpath

Heat, shade & sea air

Inland Languedoc gets properly hot at midday, so do the ramparts and castles early or late and save the shaded canal towpath or a swim for the middle of the day. The coast (Collioure, Narbonne-Plage) is a few degrees kinder with its sea breeze — an easy escape whenever the city bakes.

Storybook enough?

A floodlit fortress by night, a castle on a crag by lunch, a tree-shaded canal at your own pace, and the Mediterranean an hour away. Back to the other options whenever.
