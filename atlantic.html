The Basque coast & the golden south-west

Two easy bases by train: handsome Bordeaux and a perfect Basque bay — with Atlantic surf, the tallest dune in Europe, and Spain just down the line.

A few stone-and-river days in Bordeaux, then south to a Basque fishing bay for beaches, surf towns and a hop across to Spain: all of it on the train, all of it warm.

France's Atlantic south-west is made for this trip. Start in Bordeaux — golden squares, the great mirror-pool on the river, oysters and dunes on its doorstep at Arcachon, and the storybook hill of Saint-Émilion among the vines. Then the TGV slips down to the Basque coast: Saint-Jean-de-Luz, a half-moon of a bay with a working harbour and an old town to wander; surfy, elegant Biarritz; chocolate-scented Bayonne; and glittering San Sebastián, a short train over the border. Sea breezes, market tomatoes, gâteau basque, and every leg of it on rails.

    Down to the south-west

    Eurostar to Paris and a night in the city, then the morning TGV from Montparnasse to Bordeaux — barely two hours, with the Landes pine forest blurring past.

    Bordeaux, the golden city

    Drop bags and wander: the vast Place de la Bourse mirrored in the Miroir d'eau, honey-stone façades, the riverside promenade and the buzzy Saint-Pierre lanes for supper. A handsome, walkable opener.

  • 03

    Arcachon & the Dune du Pilat

    A 50-minute TER to the bay of Arcachon: climb the Dune du Pilat, the tallest sand dune in Europe, for views over forest and ocean, then a seafood lunch and a swim in the calm basin.

    dunebeach
  • 04

    Saint-Émilion among the vines

    A short hop to the storybook hill-village of Saint-Émilion: a monolithic church carved from the rock, steep cobbled lanes, a bell-tower view over a sea of vineyards. Cycle the green lanes if the legs fancy it.

    villageslow
  • 05

    South to the Basque coast

    Back to the station and down the coast by train — about two hours to Saint-Jean-de-Luz. Settle into the bay town, then a first evening stroll round the harbour and the half-moon beach.

    railarrive
  • 06

    Saint-Jean-de-Luz, the perfect bay

    A whole gentle day in the loveliest Basque resort: the sheltered, swimmable bay, the red-and-white old town, the covered market and Maison Louis XIV. Gâteau basque from the bakery and feet in the sand.

    baseswim
  • 07

    Biarritz & Bayonne

    Two quick hops up the line: surfy, elegant Biarritz for the Grande Plage and the wave-lashed Rocher de la Vierge, then chocolate-scented Bayonne with its riverside half-timbers and soaring cathedral.

    railsurf
  • 08

    San Sebastián, across the border

    A short train into Spain to glittering Donostia-San Sebastián: the shell-shaped La Concha bay, a funicular up Monte Igueldo, and the old town's pintxos counters — vegetable heaven, plate by plate.

    Spainfood
  • Then turn for home, rolling the trains back north with sand in your shoes and a tan from two coasts.

The plan

Two nights in Bordeaux, the rest by the sea

Good to know

The practical bits

Getting there on rails

All the way by train, with one easy night in Paris:

  • Edinburgh → London → Paris · LNER ~4h20 + Eurostar ~2h20 · overnight in Paris
  • Paris → Bordeaux · direct TGV ~2h08, roughly hourly from Montparnasse
  • Bordeaux → Saint-Jean-de-Luz · TER / Intercités ~2h05–2h30

Around the two bases

From Bordeaux: Arcachon ~50 min and Saint-Émilion ~35–40 min, both frequent TER. From Saint-Jean-de-Luz the Basque coast is tiny hops — Biarritz ~12 min, Bayonne ~25 min, Hendaye ~15 min, then the Euskotren Topo on to San Sebastián (~35 min). No car needed at all; the dune has a shuttle/short walk from Arcachon.

Eating well, meat-free

The Atlantic south-west market table:

  • Piperade, stuffed peppers and ratatouille
  • Tomato, melon and peach from the morning market
  • San Sebastián pintxos — tortilla, mushrooms, padrón peppers
  • Gâteau basque, and Bayonne chocolate the old way

Sea, surf & sun

This is the Atlantic, so the water is bracing and the surf real — Biarritz and the open beaches get proper waves, while Saint-Jean-de-Luz's bay is sheltered and calm for an easy swim. Days sit comfortably in the mid-20s with a kind sea breeze; do the Dune du Pilat early or for sunset to dodge the midday glare.

Two coasts, one ticket?

Golden Bordeaux, the tallest dune in Europe, a perfect Basque bay and a hop into Spain — all on the train. Back to the other options whenever.
